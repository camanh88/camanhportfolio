## P2

### About Lam Yen

### The brief

How a traditional gift became a daily sweet treat

Our signature mooncake was a traditional gift. It was bought once a year for Mid-Autumn, to offer on the altar or give as a gift. We wanted three things:

- **1. Own the idea.** Make it clear that ‘Cốm Xào Mooncake’ was created by Lam Yên.
- **2. Lead the category.** Position Lam Yên as the pioneer of creative cốm products.
- **3. Open the daily door.** Turn it into a daily sweet treat, not a once-a-year obligation.

## P3

### 1 · Make it a gift worth owning

### THE PACKAGING

We wanted the brand to say three things:

- **Young and creative.** A young creator’s brand.
- **A personality of its own.** A bit bold, a bit colourful, easy to spot.
- **Hanoian.** A touch of tradition, with a handmade feel.

Renewed the whole packaging, from **green-and-white** to **green-and-pink**, and added **hand-drawn illustrations** by a young artist.

#### The wrap - Been through 2 different stages

Our first Mid-Autumn, we tried to join the race of **elaborate mooncake boxes**, the kind five-star hotels make. It didn’t fit us: ++too slow++ to pack at peak season, ++too costly++ to produce, and ++far too seasonal++ for a brand aiming to be a daily treat.

So we flipped it: **a seasonal wrap** for the gift set, and simple, optimised box blanks that we refine each season while keeping them minimal.

#### The paper signature bag - Playing with pattern

At first, my team wanted to put {{pic: this illustration | pic-illustration}} straight onto the bag. But to my eye it looked like a child’s drawing, and many details like that are hard to turn into something customers recognise and remember.

So I said, **“Let’s play with pattern and our signature colour.”**

We built one from mooncake pattern, in our green and pink, softened with a gradient.

Simple, and instantly recognisable.

The pattern continued to inspire the designs of subsequent seasonal bags.

If luxury brands inspired the design of this paper bag, Marou inspired me to turn a simple box into a gift through its wrapping. 🫡

## P4

### BRAND’S IMAGE: Shaping the brand’s image

- We started with an Indochine mood: rich and elegant, aiming at a mid-to-high price.
- It is good for big campaigns, brand building, making it “a gift worth owning”
- But it’s hard for selling them daily…

## P5

### 2 · Show it the way it’s meant to be enjoyed

At first, the brand owner told me: “Our kitchen isn’t moody and traditional like on Pinterest.”

I said: “Let me figure it out. I just want to immerse myself in cốm first.”

So I spent my days in the kitchen, watching the bakers, getting to know the product. And I found something. **Our mooncake has one true way to be enjoyed: break it, and pull it apart.** No knife, no neat square slices like in every mooncake photo. Just fresh, warm, and the filling flowing out behind a crisp crust.

So that’s how I shot it, right there in the kitchen.

That ++small gesture++ became ++our signature.++ Customers and reviewers tried it and said, “Oh, I see them do it like this.” “Looks insanely delicious.”

Working side by side with the bakers, I **made a whole library of “selling content”**: _making videos, food-porn close-ups, imperfect on purpose._ It was ++cheap to produce++ and ++worked well in ads++.

It brought people closer to the product, close enough to make it part of their day.

Through consistent content and repetition, we gradually made **Lam Yên synonymous with Cốm Xào Mooncake.**

### BRAND’S IMAGE: Shaping the brand’s image

So I learned to speak in 2 registers:

- Casual “making” content for ++daily selling++
- **Indochine-styled imagery** for big campaigns and ++brand building++.

### BRAND’S VOICE: Forming the brand’s voice

Lam Yên’s founder started as an online baker, selling her cakes to people nearby. With the soul of a baker who wants to share her happiness, I leaned into that and shaped the voice of Lam Yên.

Also by talking with customers on social media, Lam Yên found its voice: a cordial, welcoming and confident baker.

### Small campaigns, new demand

### Tết 2025 · Launching Mochi, with a reel series

We introduced Cốm Mochi as a Tết gift set, since it keeps longer and is stored differently from our other cakes. But we also wanted it known as something worth eating in its own right, and to **keep confirming Lam Yên’s lead in tasty, creative cốm.**

So one of our standout pieces of communication was **a reel series**.

**The challenge**: **how do you talk about one simple product across a long run of reels?**

My answer was to ++break the cake down into its parts++. Each group of reels took one part and one angle: its origin, its inspiration, how it’s made, the flavour, the texture… And, one more thing: I wasn’t shy about ++repeating the key signatures++: “thơm ngậy”, “dai dẻo”, “không ngọt”, “không bứ”,…  I wanted people to remember about our Cốm Mochi.

**Results**

- Over two years since its launch, Cốm Mochi remains one of Lam Yên’s **top two best-sellers**, together with Cốm Xào Mooncake.
- One reel went viral and carried our ads performance for a long time (Meta).
- The other episodes kept steady engagement and brought buying enquiries from customers (Meta, TikTok).

### We have led the way in creative cốm innovation.

## P8

### 5. What changed

- From a seasonal product to a daily sweet treat.
- A brand known from the North to the South as a pioneer of creative cốm.
- Sales doubled with each Mid-Autumn.
- Content that set the pace: daily “making” content, cheap to produce, effective for both branding and marketing.
- Pattern and design approach that shaped a clear, standout brand image and gave future packaging a starting point.
- Lam Yên made its way to the U.S. Embassy in Vietnam, where KOL Mei Chan introduced it as a taste of Vietnamese tradition. [link](https://www.instagram.com/p/Ddtf8oiB15n/)

## P9

Day by day, I felt myself putting down roots in Hanoi by building Lam Yên. I worked with a focus on craftsmanship and specialty ingredients, brought my ideas to life, and sharpened my branding and marketing skills along the way. And I got to help carry our mooncakes beyond Hanoi.

When I first walked in, there was little in the kitchen and just one baker to welcome me. Now the kitchen has grown, with machines and bakers working in shifts. I watched our first store open, with a line of customers stretching out the door, and Lam Yên grow into a name associated with Hanoi’s gifting culture.

That’s where the culture lover in me found her kitchen.
